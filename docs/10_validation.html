10. Validation

Two questions are answered separately: whether the equations are solved correctly, and whether the equations and parameters describe real devices.

#10.1 Numerical verification against nextnano++

Twelve structures were solved at zero bias in both codes, using the nextnano++ free edition (100-point grid, no strain module). In the "same parameters" columns nextnano's material parameters are injected into this tool, so that only the numerics differ.

Sheet densities agree to 3% or better on every HEMT. Band edges agree to about 10 meV on structures with few interfaces. The larger differences (AlN-barrier HEMTs, multi-quantum-well emitters) are concentrated at abrupt interfaces, where the 100-point grid of the free nextnano edition cannot resolve nanometre-thick layers; they are not evidence about either solver. For the Al0.3Ga0.7N/GaN well in quantum mode, with the parameters of the earlier comparison, the ground subband was meV against meV and the 2DEG density against cm−2.

The Dreyer constants improve every GaN/AlGaN and GaN/AlN electron result, and fail for In-containing barriers and for hole gases. Two remarks:

  • For InAlN the cause is the linear alloy interpolation of a spontaneous polarization of order 1.3 C/m², taken from the original paper. A difference of two large interpolated numbers leaves the lattice-matched Al0.83In0.17N/GaN charge at about cm−2, half of what the measured sheet densities require.
  • For GaN on AlN the Dreyer bound charge is cm−2, while five samples in three papers show Hall hole densities of 4.0 to cm−2. A polarization-induced gas cannot exceed the bound charge that induces it.

Neither set is uniformly better. The Ambacher set stays the default because it fails less badly where it fails. This comparison uses this tool's elastic constants and alloy interpolation; it is a statement about the two parameter sets as implemented here, not about the underlying theory.

#10.5 Thin barriers and the surface barrier

With the fixed 3.0 eV surface barrier of the benchmark rules, the 2DEG under AlN barriers of 2-2.5 nm is overestimated by a factor of 2 to 5. Section 5.8 explains why a forward prediction is not meaningful there. Solving the inverse problem instead gives the barrier each sample requires:

Sample Barrier Measured (cm−2) Extracted
Cao & Jena 2007, AlN/GaN 2.0 nm 4.39 eV
Cao 2011, AlN/GaN 2.3 nm 4.30 eV
Cao 2008, AlN/GaN 2.3 nm 4.04 eV
Zimmermann 2008, AlN/GaN 3.5 nm 4.15 eV
Cao 2008, AlN/GaN 4.0 nm 4.14 eV
Cao & Jena 2007; Zimmermann 2009, AlN/GaN 4.0 nm 3.72 eV
Cao & Jena 2007, AlN/GaN 6.0 nm 2.87 eV
Li 2010, Al0.72Ga0.28N/AlN/GaN 2.5 nm 3.25 eV
Li 2010, Al0.72Ga0.28N/AlN/GaN 8.0 nm 1.43 eV

Three observations:

  • Seven AlN/GaN samples from five papers, 2 to 4 nm thick, give a consistent barrier of eV. The 3.0 eV assumed in the benchmark was too low for these surfaces, and that assumption, not the solver, produced the large overestimate.
  • The extracted barrier falls as the density rises (4.4 eV at , 2.9 eV at cm−2).
  • For Al0.72Ga0.28N the tool gives 3.25 eV falling to 1.43 eV. Li et al. report 3.5 eV falling to 2.2 eV for the same series from their own solver. The trend and the thin-barrier value agree; at 8 nm the tool needs a lower barrier, in the thickness range where that paper reports possible strain relaxation.

The benchmark scores in 10.3 still use the 3.0 eV rule fixed in advance. They are not rescored with the extracted barrier, since that would fit the answer to the data.

#10.7 Guidance

Use Applies to
directly sheet densities for barriers of 4 nm or more; hole gases; polarization charge; InGaN well fields; critical barrier thickness; carrier concentrations from Si and Mg doping; band gaps from 10 K upward
with a correction emission energies (subtract exciton binding); intersubband energies (60-120 meV lower); 2DEG densities (expect a Hall value 10-20% lower)
only as an inverse problem sheet densities under barriers thinner than about 4 nm (section 5.8)
not at all monolayer wells; zero-bias transition energy as a stand-in for electroluminescence of wide Al-rich wells; Pt barriers from the default metal model

#10.8 Limits of the validation

  • About half of the 78 papers added in the second round come from two groups that publish open copies. Their growth method and surface preparation are over-represented.
  • Nominally identical structures in different papers differ by 30-70% in measured sheet density. Agreement better than that cannot be established from the literature.
  • Nothing under bias has been compared with measurement: current-voltage curves, efficiency and gain are unvalidated.
  • N-polar structures (section 4.7) are implemented and tested for internal consistency but have not been compared with experiment. Tunnel junctions are outside the model.
  • The benchmark was assembled with machine assistance. The device rows were checked against their papers a second time, but the tables have not been reviewed by an independent expert.
