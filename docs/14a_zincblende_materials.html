15. Zincblende arsenides and phosphides

Chapters 2 to 4 describe the wurtzite nitrides. The same solver also handles the cubic (zincblende) arsenides and phosphides of Al, Ga and In, grown along [001]. Everything from chapter 5 onwards (electrostatics, quantum mechanics, currents, optics, algorithms) applies unchanged; only the material model of this chapter differs. A structure uses one crystal system: wurtzite and zincblende layers cannot be mixed in a stack.

There is no polarization. A zincblende crystal has no spontaneous polarization, and its only piezoelectric constant, , couples to shear strain, which biaxial strain on (001) does not contain. The fixed charge of chapter 4 is therefore zero, and band bending comes from doping and contacts alone.

#15.10 What is left out

  • Orientations other than (001), and with them piezoelectric fields in (111) structures.
  • Splitting of the X valleys under strain; each valley shifts hydrostatically only.
  • Coupling between valleys. A electron in a GaAs well sees the lowest valley of the barrier, which for AlAs or Al-rich AlGaAs is X and lower than the barrier it actually confines against. Quantum wells with barriers below about 40 % Al are not affected.
  • Valley degeneracy in the quantum-mechanical density when the lowest valley is X or L (the classical density (15.4) includes it).
  • Antimonides, dilute nitrides and bismides.
  • DX centres in AlGaAs, and the composition dependence of acceptor levels beyond (15.2).
  • Alloy scattering in the mobility.

#15.11 Comparison with published experiments

Twenty-five papers on arsenide and phosphide lasers, light-emitting diodes, quantum wells, intersubband detectors, band offsets and bulk alloys were rebuilt with the default model and scored with the rubric of section 10.2: 10 points within 30 meV (or 10 %), 9 within 60 meV, 8 within 100 meV, down to 1. The rules were fixed before any simulation: undoped flat-band wells, transition from the first electron to the top valence subband, no exciton binding, laser and LED lines taken as the transition energy, and for low-temperature lines the 300 K confinement energy plus the gap shift of the well alloy.

What this supports.

  • Bulk gaps and band offsets (15 papers, mostly within 30 meV). These are close to the measurements the parameter set was compiled from, so they confirm that the interpolation (15.2) and the alignment (15.5) are implemented correctly more than they test them independently. The two entries marked * were fitted directly.
  • Quantum wells of ordinary width (4 to 8 nm): the 1.53 µm InGaAs/InP laser of Tsang is reproduced to 1 meV, the 980 nm strained InGaAs/GaAs well to 22 meV, the 680 nm GaInP laser to 10 meV. These test strain, offsets and the Schrödinger solver together and were not fitted.
  • Intersubband transitions in GaAs wells: 1 and 3 meV from West and Eglash.

Where it deviates.

  • Wells of one to three monolayers (Marsh, Tsang and Schubert): 150 to 180 meV too high. An envelope-function model with an abrupt interface overestimates confinement in a 0.6 to 1 nm well; real interfaces are graded over a comparable distance.
  • Narrow-gap wells, excited states: E2 − E1 of the InGaAs/InAlAs well is 60 meV too high because the conduction band is taken as parabolic.
  • GaInP: the 633 nm laser comes out 82 meV too high and the GaInP/GaAs offsets are 35 to 70 meV off. GaInP grown by MOCVD is partly ordered, which lowers its gap by up to 50 meV (Gomyo); the model is the random alloy.
  • AlGaAs gap: 43 meV above the Casey and Panish line, because the parameter set uses the later Aspnes bowing. The crossover composition, 0.42, is above the measured 0.385.
  • InGaAs/InAlAs offsets: within 22 meV of the C-V value of People, but 80 to 100 meV from the optical values of Weiner. The two experiments disagree with each other by 60 meV.

Limits of this benchmark. Twenty-five papers is a quarter of the nitride set. There is one electron-gas value, no hole gas, no current-voltage or efficiency data, and nothing on the recombination coefficients of section 15.9. Three structures (marked Low in the full tables) had to be completed with standard layer values because their abstracts do not give them. The mean score is high partly because most bulk values are near the source data of the parameter set.
