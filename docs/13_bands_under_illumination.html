13. Bands under illumination

Light of one wavelength enters through the top surface and generates electron-hole pairs where it is absorbed. This chapter states how the generation is computed, how it enters the current equations of chapter 7, and how the structure is brought to the state it takes with no external circuit.

The implementation is physics/illumination.py; the generation term is in physics/dd_newton.py.

#13.5 Boundaries

The solver has contacts only (5.16) to (5.18), so the two ends are represented as:

End Represented by Meaning
bottom the bottom contact the neutral substrate
top the top contact the free surface

A Schottky top contact with barrier is a surface whose Fermi level is pinned below the conduction band; an ohmic one is a flat-band surface.

WarningBoth quasi-Fermi levels are pinned together at the top node. This is a surface that recombines carriers infinitely fast. The light is absorbed within about nm of the surface while carriers diffuse several times further, so most of them reach the surface and are lost. The quasi-Fermi level splitting near the surface is therefore a lower bound. A finite surface recombination velocity is not available in the solver.

At growth temperature the thermionic current over the surface barrier is so large that the same light cannot build a photovoltage: the bands do not move. What the light changes there is the minority-carrier density, which is the subject of chapter 14.

These results are internally consistent ((13.6) is met to of the short-circuit current, and the dark state is recovered below the gap). They have not been compared with a measurement.
