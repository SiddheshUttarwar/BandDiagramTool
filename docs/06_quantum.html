6. Quantum mechanics

The classical densities (5.4)-(5.5) depend only on the local band edge. They put the peak of a 2DEG exactly at the interface, give zero density inside a barrier, and know nothing of quantised levels. In the quantum modes the tool solves for the confined states of a designated region with an eight-band k·p Hamiltonian and uses them for its charge.

The solver output lists the nodes where this happened.

Exact reductions. For an in-plane wave vector along , the vertical plane containing it is a mirror plane of the axial Hamiltonian. In the eigenbasis of that mirror the Hamiltonian splits into two 4×4 blocks at every node, which are solved separately; a constant diagonal phase makes each block real symmetric. Zincblende is averaged over in-plane rotations (the axial approximation, which removes the cubic warping); wurtzite is isotropic in-plane already. Eigenpairs in an energy window come from shift-invert Lanczos, by spectrum slicing when the window holds many states.

Outside the region carriers are classical. Solved over a whole device, a thick doped contact layer would act as a very wide well with a dense ladder of states.

In an ordinary well every conduction state lies above and every valence state below the mid-gap of each node it occupies, and (6.6) is the usual per-state assignment. In a broken-gap structure it is not: a strong polarization field can put on one side of a layer below on the other (AlN/InN/AlN), and a single state is then conduction-like at one end and valence-like at the other. Labelling whole states by their weight counted the valence-like end as electrons and the conduction-like end as holes, which gave holes where and kept the self-consistent loop from converging; (6.6) assigns each end to its own carrier.

Energy windows. States are computed in a conduction window from just below the lowest Γ edge to above , and a valence window from just above the highest down to below , so the neglected occupation is below . Under bias the window covers every node's own band edge, because the current crosses every node. When the two windows overlap (broken gap) one window holds every state once.

Indirect valleys. k·p contains only the Γ conduction band. The X and L electrons of indirect zincblende alloys (AlAs, GaP, Al-rich AlGaAs) are single-band effective-mass problems on their own band edges, added to (6.4), as in nextnano++.

The band diagram, the wavefunction plot and the optics use the states: the lowest conduction and highest valence levels, each a Kramers pair, with summed over the components and each hole level labelled by its heavy-, light- or crystal-field character.
