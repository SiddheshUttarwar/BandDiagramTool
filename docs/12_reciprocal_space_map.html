12. Reciprocal space map

The strain profile of chapter 3 fixes the lattice of every grid cell, and with it where each layer scatters X-rays. This chapter states how the tool turns a structure into a simulated reciprocal space map. It is post-processing: it needs the composition and strain arrays only, not a solved potential.

The implementation is physics/rsm.py.

Rathkanthiwar et al., Appl. Phys. Lett. 120, 202105 (2022), report (00.4) and (10.5) maps of 1.8 µm Al0.6Ga0.4N on 0.5 µm AlN on an AlN substrate, and conclude that the layer is pseudomorphic.

A fit of composition and relaxation to the two maps gives and %. The offset in between the AlGaN and AlN peaks is −0.34 units, against −28 for full relaxation, and +0.23 on the symmetric map where it must be zero.

NoteThis confirms the reading of the published data. It is not independent evidence of the growth mode: the tool takes the strain state as an input and does not predict whether a layer relaxes. The published figure has no tick labels; the scale was taken as equal on both axes, which makes the ticks 5 units apart.

The biaxial stress of the fitted strain, by (3.6) with , of Vurgaftman and Meyer, is −4.5 GPa. The paper reports −3.6 GPa from wafer curvature for this sample. The difference is not explained.

#12.8 Limits

Not included Consequence
dynamical diffraction the peak of a thick, perfect layer is too tall and too narrow
absorption no attenuation of deep layers
mosaic tilt and twist, finite lateral coherence peak widths are the resolution function, not the sample's
diffuse scattering no intensity between rods
the substrate below the simulated stack only the layers entered scatter
atomic form factors and Debye-Waller factors relative intensities by (12.5) only

Positions are as reliable as the lattice and elastic constants of chapter 2. Intensities and line shapes have not been compared with a measurement.
