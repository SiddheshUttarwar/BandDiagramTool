11. Limits, and comparison with nextnano++

#11.1 Summary of assumptions

#11.2 What is not modelled

Structure and materials

  • two- and three-dimensional geometry: gates, mesas, current spreading, nanowires, dots
  • semi-polar and non-polar orientations; zincblende on orientations other than (001)
  • nitride alloys beyond Al-In-Ga-N (ScAlN, BAlN); antimonides, group IV and II-VI materials; dielectrics, metals as layers
  • wurtzite and zincblende layers in one structure
  • alloy disorder, interface roughness, indium clustering
  • relaxation, critical thickness, cracking, dislocations

Electrostatics

  • temperature dependence of masses, dielectric constants and dopant levels
  • compensation, native defects, DX centres, impurity bands
  • buffer traps (Fe, C); surface donors unless entered explicitly
  • band-gap narrowing at high doping

Quantum mechanics

  • multi-band k·p, valence-band mixing, nonparabolicity
  • excitons and many-body effects
  • tunnelling, resonant states, superlattice minibands

Transport

  • thermionic emission and tunnelling at heterobarriers
  • field-, doping- and temperature-dependent mobility; velocity saturation
  • impact ionization, breakdown, optical generation
  • self-heating; transient and small-signal response

Optics

  • optical modes, confinement factor, threshold, extraction
  • spontaneous emission spectra and efficiency
  • in-plane dispersion in the gain; polarization selection rules

#11.3 Comparison with nextnano++

nextnano++ is a commercial Schrödinger-Poisson-current solver. The numerical agreement of the two codes on identical inputs is documented in section 10.1. The table compares the models, based on the nextnano++ model reference.

Dimensions 1D, 2D, 3D 1D
Materials group IV, III-V, II-VI; zincblende and wurtzite; database editable wurtzite Al-In-Ga-N and zincblende Al-In-Ga-As-P (chapter 15); parameters in source
Crystal orientation arbitrary, by Miller indices c-plane, metal- or N-polar (4.11)
Alloy interpolation linear, quadratic and cubic schemes; alloys of up to eight components linear with pairwise bowing; three nitride binaries, six arsenide and phosphide binaries (15.2)

Where the two give the same answer. Band diagrams, sheet densities and single-band subband energies of c-plane nitride layer stacks, when the same parameters are used.

Where nextnano++ should be preferred. Anything needing valence-band mixing (polarization of emission, accurate hole subbands, gain magnitude), crystal orientations other than c-plane, lateral geometry, or materials outside Al-In-Ga-N.

What this tool adds. Composition-dependent Si and Mg ionization energies for the nitrides built in; polarization charge that is exactly conservative on non-uniform grids; a fully coupled drift-diffusion Newton solver with a current-conservation diagnostic; a choice of two polarization parameter sets and a comparison of them on measured devices (section 10.4); extraction of the surface barrier from a measured sheet density (section 5.8); and validation tables against 102 published nitride experiments (chapter 10 and Appendix A).

#11.4 Related modules

physics/defects/ contains a point-defect formation-energy calculator, a phenomenological DX-centre model, a surface-pinning model and a step-flow growth profile. They evaluate defect thermodynamics on a converged band diagram. They do not contribute charge to the solution described in this reference.
